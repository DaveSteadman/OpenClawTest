## 1. Introduction

This report provides an executive overview of BAE Systems’ recent performance, contractual achievements, and workforce dynamics as of 22 February 2026. It synthesises key events, strategic implications, opportunities, and risks to inform senior decision‑makers and stakeholders.

## 2. Key Events

BAE Systems reported record sales of £30.66 billion for 2025, a 10% increase year‑on‑year, and underlying earnings before interest and taxes of £3.32 billion, up 12% from 2024. The order backlog reached a record £83.6 billion, with order intake at £36.8 billion, underscoring sustained demand for its defence platforms. In the same period, the company secured more than £27 billion in contracts, including a €4 billion contract to build 20 Eurofighter Typhoon aircraft for Turkey, a £453.5 million radar contract for UK Typhoons, and a $145 million Pentagon contract for anti‑drone systems. Concurrently, BAE’s Lancashire factories experienced a strike led by Unite, with workers demanding a 5.2% pay rise versus the company’s 3.7% offer, reflecting tensions between labour costs and profitability.

## 3. Strategic Implications

The surge in contracts and record backlog positions BAE to capitalize on the ‘new era’ of defence spending driven by geopolitical uncertainty and NATO modernization programmes. The company’s forecast of 9‑11% earnings growth and 7‑9% sales growth for 2026 indicates confidence in sustaining momentum. However, the labour strike highlights potential operational disruptions that could erode delivery schedules and strain customer confidence. BAE’s continued investment in technology and selective acquisitions aligns with a strategy to maintain competitive advantage in advanced conventional and disruptive systems.

## 4. Opportunities

BAE can leverage its robust order book to expand into emerging markets, particularly in the European and Middle Eastern regions, where defence budgets are rising. The advanced radar and anti‑drone contracts signal growth in electronic warfare and cyber‑defence sectors, offering diversification beyond traditional platforms. Capital allocation plans, including £1.5 billion in dividends and share buybacks, can enhance shareholder value while funding R&D for next‑generation weapons. Strategic partnerships with allies, such as the UK‑Norway collaboration on the Dreadnought programme, present avenues for joint development and shared risk.

## 5. Risks

Labor unrest at key manufacturing sites poses a risk of production delays, potentially impacting delivery commitments and contractual penalties. Wage negotiations may erode profit margins if higher pay increases are conceded. Geopolitical volatility could alter defence budgets, affecting demand for BAE’s products. Supply chain constraints, particularly for advanced electronics and aerospace components, may increase costs and extend lead times. Finally, reliance on large, long‑term contracts exposes BAE to concentration risk if key customers reduce orders or shift to alternative suppliers.

## 6. Summary

BAE Systems has achieved record financial performance and secured substantial contracts, reinforcing its position as Europe’s largest defence contractor. The company’s growth outlook remains positive, supported by a strong backlog and strategic investments in technology. Nonetheless, labour disputes and geopolitical uncertainties present operational and financial risks that must be managed proactively. By capitalising on emerging defence trends and maintaining robust risk mitigation strategies, BAE can sustain its competitive edge and deliver value to shareholders.
