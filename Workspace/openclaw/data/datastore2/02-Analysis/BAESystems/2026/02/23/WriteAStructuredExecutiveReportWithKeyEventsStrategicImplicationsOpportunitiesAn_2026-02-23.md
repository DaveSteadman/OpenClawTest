## 1. Introduction

This executive report provides a concise analysis of BAE Systems’ recent performance, contractual achievements, workforce dynamics, and strategic positioning as of 23 February 2026.

## 2. Record Financial Performance

BAE Systems reported underlying earnings before interest and taxes of £3.32 billion for 2025, a 12% increase from 2024, while sales rose 10% to a record £30.66 billion.

The company’s order backlog reached a record £83.6 billion, and it forecast 2026 sales growth of 7% to 9% with underlying earnings growth of 9% to 11%.

These figures underscore a robust financial foundation that supports continued investment and shareholder returns.

## 3. Contract Portfolio and Growth Opportunities

In 2025 BAE secured over £27 billion in contracts across aviation, naval, ground, and electronic systems, including a €4 billion contract to build 20 Eurofighter Typhoon aircraft for Turkey with deliveries slated for 2030.

Additional high‑value contracts include a £453.5 million UK Ministry of Defence award for advanced radar production for Typhoon aircraft and a $145 million U.S. Air Force contract for dual‑mode APKWS upgrades.

The breadth of these contracts positions BAE to capture sustained demand from NATO modernization programs and global defense spending increases.

## 4. Workforce Disputes and Operational Risks

Unite union members at BAE’s Lancashire sites have been on strike, demanding a 5.2% pay increase to offset last year’s below‑inflation raises, while BAE offered 3.7% for 2026.

The strike involves approximately 5,000 workers with average salaries of £50,000, and BAE has highlighted that the majority of employees continue to work, implementing contingency plans to mitigate disruption.

Prolonged labor unrest could impact production timelines, cost structures, and the company’s reputation among defense contractors and clients.

## 5. Strategic Positioning in Global Defense Landscape

BAE’s record order intake and backlog, combined with its diversified product portfolio, reinforce its status as Europe’s largest defense contractor and the seventh‑largest globally.

CEO Charles Woodburn emphasizes BAE’s readiness to deliver both conventional systems and disruptive technologies amid escalating security challenges, positioning the firm for long‑term growth.

The company’s capital allocation strategy, including significant investment in technology and selective acquisitions, aims to sustain competitive advantage and shareholder value.

## 6. Summary

BAE Systems demonstrates strong financial performance and a growing contract portfolio, reinforcing its leadership in the defense sector.

However, workforce disputes present operational risks that could affect production and cost efficiency.

Strategic focus on advanced technologies and global defense spending trends offers significant growth opportunities, provided labor relations are resolved and execution remains on schedule.
