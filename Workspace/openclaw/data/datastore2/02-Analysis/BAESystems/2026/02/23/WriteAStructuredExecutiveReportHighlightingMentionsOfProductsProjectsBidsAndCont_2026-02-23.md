## 1. Introduction

BAE Systems, the United Kingdom's largest defence contractor, reported significant financial achievements and contract wins in 2025, positioning itself for continued growth amid heightened global defence spending.

## 2. Record Sales and Financial Performance

The company announced underlying earnings before interest and taxes of £3.32 billion for 2025, a 12 % increase from 2024, while sales rose 10 % to a record £30.66 billion. Its order backlog reached £83.6 billion, up £5.8 billion from the previous year, reflecting strong demand for its aircraft, weapons, and naval platforms.

## 3. Contract Awards and Projects

In 2025 BAE secured more than £27 billion ($35 billion) in contracts across aviation, naval, ground, and electronic systems. Notable awards include a four‑billion euro contract to build 20 Eurofighter Typhoon aircraft for Turkey with deliveries beginning in 2030, a £453.5 million contract for a new advanced radar for UK Typhoon aircraft, a $145 million sole‑source contract for dual‑mode APKWS upgrade, and a $1.7 billion five‑year contract to supply laser‑guidance kits to the U.S. Navy.

## 4. Workforce and Labor Issues

Despite record performance, BAE faced industrial action at its Lancashire sites, where 5,000 workers represented by Unite demanded higher pay. The union cited a 3.6 % raise for higher‑skilled staff and a 4.2 % rise for factory floor workers, while striking staff sought a 5.2 % increase. BAE offered 3.7 % raises for 2026, describing the offer as fair and balancing competitiveness with customer affordability.

## 5. Strategic Positioning and Future Outlook

CEO Charles Woodburn highlighted the "new era" of defence spending driven by escalating security challenges, noting that BAE’s record order backlog and investment in technology position the company to deliver advanced conventional systems and disruptive technologies. The firm forecasts sales growth of 7 % to 9 % in 2026 and cumulative free cash flow exceeding £6 billion through 2028.

## 6. Summary

BAE Systems’ 2025 performance, marked by record sales, a robust order backlog, and substantial contract wins across multiple domains, underscores its strategic readiness to capitalize on the global ramp‑up in defence spending, while internal labour disputes highlight ongoing challenges to workforce stability.
