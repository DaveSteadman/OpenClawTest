## 1. Introduction

This report provides an executive overview of Saab’s recent corporate activities and market developments as of 22 February 2026. It synthesises key events, strategic implications, opportunities, and risks derived from the latest press releases, contract awards, and financial results. The objective is to inform stakeholders of Saab’s current positioning and future prospects within the global defence and security landscape.

## 2. Key Events

On 18 February 2026, Saab’s President and CEO Micael Johansson sold 14,000 shares and Deputy CEO Anders Carp sold 4,500 shares to pay tax on long‑term incentive allocations, leaving Johansson with 343,299 shares and Carp with 95,798 shares. This transaction reflects internal capital management but may influence shareholder perception.

On 13 February 2026, Saab signed a Memorandum of Understanding with the Joint Stock Company “Ukrainian Defense Industry” (JSC UDI) at the Munich Security Conference to collaborate on aviation and airborne surveillance, thereby strengthening Ukraine’s defence capabilities.

The company reported record order bookings for 2025, with strong sales, operating income, and cash flow, and upgraded its medium‑term organic sales growth target. The Q4 2025 results were presented by CEO Micael Johansson and CFO Anna Wijkander, underscoring robust financial performance.

Recent contract awards include a SEK 1.5 billion Trackfire Remote Weapon Station order from the Swedish Defence Materiel Administration, a Poland SIGINT ship launch, a NATO 9AIR C4I system order valued at ~250 million SEK, and a U.S. Army Giraffe 1X radar contract worth ~46 million USD. These contracts demonstrate Saab’s expanding footprint across Europe and the United States.

Saab also secured a $276 million Swedish contract to study next‑generation fighter concepts, covering both crewed and unmanned platforms, and a U.S. contract for the SHIELD IDIQ program, highlighting its role in advanced air command and control and homeland defense.

## 3. Strategic Implications

The MoU with JSC UDI positions Saab as a key partner in Eastern European defence, potentially opening new markets and reinforcing its reputation for rapid technology transfer.

Record order bookings and upgraded growth targets signal that Saab is capitalising on heightened European defence spending triggered by the Ukraine conflict, strengthening its competitive advantage in the Gripen and other platforms.

The diverse contract portfolio across NATO, U.S., and Swedish customers indicates a broadening of Saab’s customer base, reducing reliance on any single market and enhancing resilience to geopolitical shifts.

The sale of shares by senior executives may be viewed as a prudent tax strategy but could also raise concerns about insider confidence, necessitating transparent communication to maintain investor trust.

The focus on future fighter studies and advanced C4I systems aligns with long‑term strategic priorities to innovate beyond the current Gripen generation, ensuring relevance in the 2030s and beyond.

## 4. Opportunities

Expanding collaboration with Ukrainian defence firms offers access to Eastern European markets and potential joint development projects in aviation and surveillance technologies.

The growing demand for remote weapon stations and advanced C4I systems presents opportunities to deepen penetration in NATO member states and partner nations seeking integrated air defence solutions.

The U.S. SHIELD IDIQ contract and Giraffe 1X radar order open avenues for further penetration into the U.S. defense market, especially in homeland security and training sectors.

The next‑generation fighter studies contract provides a platform for Saab to influence the design of future air combat systems, potentially securing long‑term procurement contracts and establishing a foothold in the sixth‑generation fighter arena.

Leveraging the strong order backlog and upgraded growth outlook, Saab can invest in capacity expansion, research and development, and supply chain resilience to support future contracts.

## 5. Risks

Geopolitical tensions, particularly in Eastern Europe, could affect the stability of partnerships and the execution of joint projects with Ukrainian entities.

Fluctuations in defence budgets, especially in the U.S. and European markets, may impact the timing and size of future orders, affecting revenue projections.

The sale of shares by top executives, while tax‑related, may be perceived negatively by investors, potentially affecting share price and market sentiment.

Supply chain constraints, especially for advanced electronics and sensors, could delay delivery schedules for high‑profile contracts such as the 9AIR C4I system and the Trackfire RWS.

Regulatory and export control complexities in cross‑border defence collaborations may introduce compliance risks and potential delays in project timelines.

## 6. Summary

Saab’s recent activities demonstrate a robust growth trajectory, underpinned by record order bookings, strategic partnerships, and a diversified contract portfolio across Europe and the United States.

The company is strategically positioning itself to benefit from increased defence spending, particularly in NATO and U.S. markets, while expanding its technological footprint through collaborations in Ukraine and future fighter studies.

Opportunities lie in deepening market penetration, advancing technology transfer, and securing long‑term procurement contracts for next‑generation systems.

Risks include geopolitical volatility, budgetary uncertainties, supply chain constraints, and potential investor perception challenges stemming from executive share sales.

Overall, Saab appears well‑placed to sustain growth and innovation, provided it manages the identified risks and continues to leverage its strong global presence and technological capabilities.
