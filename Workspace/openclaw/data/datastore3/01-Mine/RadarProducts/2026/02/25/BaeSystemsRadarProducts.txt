  BAE Systems’ Cowes facility has re-emerged as one of the UK’s most important centres for radar engineering, anchoring a rapidly expanding national effort to deliver next-generation sensors. This second article examines the site’s evolution, its growing workforce and the technologies now shaping Britain’s future radar capability. Radar development at Cowes began during the Second World War, when several small electronics workshops around the Isle of Wight were absorbed into the rapid expansion of Britain’s radar effort. The island offered both industrial capacity and a degree of isolation suited to sensitive defence programmes. The factory that continues this work today was built by the Decca radar company in 1964 on the old Somerton airfield at Cowes, primarily for the manufacture of airfield radars. In 1965, Plessey bought out Decca and Cowes grew into a major centre for naval and air-defence radar engineering, producing surface-search radars, naval IFF systems, missile-guidance components and early phased-array technologies. It became central to the development of sensors linked to naval weapon systems such as Seaslug and Sea Dart, and by the late Cold War, the facility had earned a reputation as one of Britain’s premier defence-electronics sites.
