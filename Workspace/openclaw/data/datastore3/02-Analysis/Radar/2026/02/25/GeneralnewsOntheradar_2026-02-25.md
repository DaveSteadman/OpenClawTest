## 2. Integrated Air and Missile Defense Modernization

The U.S. Army awarded Lockheed Martin a $33.8 million cost‑plus‑incentive‑fee contract to support ground and flight testing of the Lower Tier Air and Missile Defense Sensor (LTAMDS) integrated with PAC‑3 interceptors, a critical step toward fielding a 360‑degree radar for Patriot systems.

Raytheon’s LTAMDS replaces the legacy Patriot radar with a three‑array design that delivers more than double the power of the previous sensor, enabling detection of low‑flying cruise missiles, maneuvering ballistic threats, and swarming drones across the full azimuth.

The contract underscores a shift from sensor‑only trials to a holistic engagement stack, ensuring that radar, command‑and‑control, launchers, and interceptor software operate in concert, thereby enhancing a customer’s layered defense and reducing response time against multi‑axis attacks.

## 3. Next‑Generation Radar Production and Fielding

Lockheed Martin delivered the first Sentinel A4 radar from Low‑Rate Initial Production (LRIP‑2) to the U.S. Army, completing the initial operational test phase and moving the system toward full‑rate production; Sentinel A4 expands battlespace awareness against cruise missiles, UAVs, and rotary‑wing threats in complex environments.

In the United Kingdom, Indra secured a £13.1 million contract to supply its Lanza Long Tactical Range‑25 radar to the RAF’s Battlespace Management Force, a compact system capable of rapid worldwide deployment via C‑130 aircraft and engineered to mitigate wind‑farm clutter while offering dual‑speed rotation for air‑traffic radar service.

BAE Systems received a $612 million contract to equip RAF Typhoon fighter jets with a new advanced AESA radar, reinforcing air‑to‑air and air‑to‑ground sensing capabilities for the United Kingdom’s frontline combat aircraft.

Collectively, these deliveries provide customers with scalable, high‑power radar architectures that improve detection range, target discrimination, and multi‑role flexibility, supporting both static homeland defense and expeditionary operations.

## 4. Aerostat and Tethered Radar Early Warning

Poland’s 37th Radiotechnical Battalion is fielding the Barbara tethered‑aerostat radar system under a $960 million U.S. Foreign Military Sale, extending low‑altitude surveillance hundreds of kilometres along the nation’s eastern NATO border.

The aerostat platform restores line‑of‑sight coverage at altitudes where conventional ground radars are limited, while passive listening stations complement the radar by geolocating emitters without radiating, complicating adversary jamming and anti‑radiation tactics.

Infrastructure construction at sites such as Kurzyna Wielka is underway, with full operational capability expected by the end of 2027, delivering Poland with persistent early‑warning coverage and a strategic ISR layer that enhances NATO’s collective air defense posture.

## 5. Deep Space and Space Surveillance Radar

The Deep Space Advanced Radar Capability (DARC) programme, a trilateral effort among the United Kingdom, United States, and Australia, aims to detect, identify, and track objects in Earth orbit, including satellites, debris, and asteroids.

The UK plans to locate its DARC site at the former Cawdor Barracks in Pembrokeshire, with an environmental impact assessment completed and public consultation closing on 23 March 2026.

By providing high‑precision orbital tracking, DARC safeguards space‑based services critical to national infrastructure and offers customers—both military and civilian—enhanced situational awareness for collision avoidance and space‑domain operations.

## 6. Tactical Data Link and Multi‑Function Radios

BAE Systems, through its joint venture Data Link Solutions with Collins Aerospace (RTX), secured a $248 million U.S. Navy contract to produce Multifunctional Information Distribution System Joint Tactical Radio System (MIDS JTRS) terminals, delivering jam‑resistant Link 16 connectivity for air, land, and maritime platforms.

The software‑defined, four‑channel radios support Link 16 and additional waveforms, enabling coalition forces to share real‑time tactical data across domains, thereby improving interoperability and command‑and‑control fidelity.

For customers, the MIDS JTRS solution adds a resilient, multi‑band communications layer that integrates seamlessly with existing radar and sensor networks, enhancing the speed and accuracy of threat dissemination in contested environments.
